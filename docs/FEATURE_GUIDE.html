Chandralign: every feature, explained for judge Q&A

This guide covers what we are building, why each piece exists, how we are building it, and what already exists in the world. It uses plain language throughout, so anyone on the team can answer a judge's question with confidence.

1 · Start here

This guide is built from two sources only: our PLAN.md and FEATURES.csv (what we build and how), and the SIH26166 Deep Technical Research report (what already exists: papers, competitors, tools, data). No other research was added.

One rule before anything else: every feature is still a plan. All 85 rows in FEATURES.csv are marked TODO, and we have not measured any result yet. If a judge asks for a number we haven't measured, the answer is "we measure that at build time and report whatever it is." Never quote a published paper's number as our own. This is honesty rule H1/H5 and it is enforced by tests.

How to read a feature card

  • What it is: the feature in plain words.
  • How we build it: the library, algorithm or method we use.
  • What exists / why ours: what is already out there (from the research PDF), and why we still need this piece.
  • Say this: a short line you can say to a judge.
  • Proof it works: the acceptance test from FEATURES.csv. A feature is only "done" when this test passes.

Badges: ★ our differentiator marks the pieces nobody has built before. PS asks for this marks features the problem statement asks for directly. P0 must P1 should P2 stretch show priority.

2 · The problem, in simple words

Image registration means lining up two photos of the same place so that each point in one sits exactly on the same point in the other. The problem statement gives the two roles names:

  • Source image (moving): a Chandrayaan-2 photo from one of its three cameras (OHRC, TMC-2 or IIRS). We bend and shift this one.
  • Reference image (fixed): a trusted outside photo of the same ground, from NASA's LRO (NAC or WAC cameras) or Japan's SELENE/Kaguya. It stays still.

To line them up, we find the same spots in both images (match points), work out the mathematical shape change between them (the transform), then warp the source to fit the reference.

Why this is hard on the Moon

The problem statement names three challenges:

Sun angle (illumination)

The Moon has no air, so shadows are pitch black and sharp. If the Sun is in a different place in the two photos, the same crater looks completely different. Near the poles the Sun is very low and shadows are huge.

Scale (resolution)

One OHRC pixel covers 0.25 m of ground. One IIRS pixel covers about 80 m, which is about 320 times bigger. A single IIRS pixel can hold a whole OHRC image's worth of detail.

Sensor type (multimodal)

OHRC and TMC-2 are black-and-white cameras. IIRS is an infrared spectrometer with about 250 colour bands. The same ground can have different brightness in each band.

Viewpoint

Different orbits and pushbroom cameras mean the images can be rotated and tilted relative to each other.

What the problem statement wants delivered

  • A generic piece of software: one system for all cameras, not three hand-tuned ones.
  • A registered product (the aligned image) plus the corresponding match points.
  • Sub-pixel accuracy: the error must be smaller than one pixel of the source image.
  • Match points spread evenly across the image, not bunched in one corner.
  • Evaluation metrics, such as RMSE, inlier count and inlier ratio.

The key reading of the problem statement. Its own Dataset field lists Chandrayaan-2 OHRC, TMC-2 and IIRS as the source, and LRO NAC and SELENE images as the reference. It gives no link for matching the three Chandrayaan-2 cameras against each other. So our main target is Chandrayaan-2 against an external reference. Matching the cameras to each other is a useful tool we use along the way, not the main goal.

4 · What already exists

Everything in this section comes from the research PDF.

Published research

  • arXiv:2509.04775 (also IEEE INGARSS 2024) is the most important paper. It is the only one that compares SIFT, ASIFT, AKAZE, RIFT2 and SuperGlue on real Chandrayaan-2 data (OHRC↔NAC, IIRS↔WAC). SuperGlue gave the lowest error and the fastest runtime (0.57–0.92 px, equatorial to polar). The classical methods (SIFT, AKAZE) got worse specifically at the lunar poles. The paper never mentions TMC-2, and it doesn't test OHRC↔WAC, IIRS↔NAC or SELENE.
  • preprints.org 2025 (SIFT-based IIRS registration) registers IIRS to LRO WAC with SIFT, with error below one IIRS pixel. It covers one pair with a classical method only.
  • MultiResSAR (arXiv:2502.01002, 2025) is an Earth radar-optical benchmark that tested 16 algorithms. Classical RIFT beat the best deep method (66.5% vs 40.6%) at high resolution. This is why we don't say "AI always wins".
  • Geo-LoFTR ablation: vanilla LoFTR fails almost completely at very low sun elevation (EL = 2°) on Mars-like terrain.
  • Photoclinometry-assisted matching (Liu & Wu 2020) and multiview shape-from-shading (Alexandrov & Beyer 2018) use known sun geometry to help matching. They are 3D-reconstruction methods, not general 2D matchers.
  • Crater-neighbourhood matching (Remote Sensing 2025) uses craters as landmarks that don't change with lighting. It has not been tested on Chandrayaan-2.
  • Coarse-to-fine, 1×–50× resolution (Remote Sensing 2019) is the closest published method for big scale gaps, but it stops at 50×, not 320×.
  • No existing benchmark (HPatches, MegaDepth, IMC, SEN1-2, and others) fits lunar, multi-sensor data with public ground truth.

5 · What we claim, and what we don't

Our claim, word for word: A generic, instrument-agnostic registration pipeline that registers any Chandrayaan-2 instrument against either named external reference. It is validated honestly on the two neglected axes, TMC-2 and SELENE, and it reproduces the two already-solved pairs (OHRC↔NAC, IIRS↔WAC) as a credibility floor.

We do not claim a new matching algorithm. What is new is the combination, the validation focus and the honesty discipline:

  1. A real, tested PDS3/PDS4 reader, which is the part most competitors fake.
  2. Real TMC-2 and SELENE coverage using real downloaded products, not synthetic stand-ins.
  3. Automatic method selection plus physics-guided filtering: classical RIFT for different sensors, LightGlue for extreme lighting, and a step-by-step cascade across the 320:1 scale gap.
  4. Control-gated honest validation: null tests, confidence tiers and published failures.

What we deliberately don't build

  • Direct OHRC↔IIRS matching without steps in between. Two teams already failed at it.
  • A model "fine-tuned" for 1 epoch and marketed as fine-tuned.
  • SuperPoint, SuperGlue or R2D2 in the shipped software, because of their non-commercial licences.
  • Vanilla LoFTR for extreme low-sun images.
  • A beautiful README over thin code.

6 · How the pipeline flows

One run goes through these stages in order. The feature IDs link each stage to the cards in section 7. Highlighted stages are our differentiators.

  1. Read the real filesRead PDS4/PDS3 labels and pixels, detect the camera, cut the image into tiles. DATA-01 … DATA-13
  2. Work out the physicsSun angles, terrain slope from elevation maps, ground overlap check, scale pre-check. GEO-01 … GEO-07
  3. Prepare the imagesContrast boost, phase-congruency transform, IIRS composite, shadow mask, terrain texture scores. PREP-01 … PREP-08
  4. GATE A · a clean, geometry-tagged tile pair (ImagePlane) is handed over
  5. Scale-bridging cascadeCross large resolution gaps in steps (OHRC → TMC-2 → SELENE TC / IIRS → LRO), never in one jump. MATCH-10, MATCH-11
  6. Automatic matcher choicePick LightGlue, LoFTR, RIFT2 or the cascade based on lighting difference, scale and sensor type. MATCH-01 … MATCH-09, MATCH-12 … MATCH-15
  7. Fit the transformMAGSAC++ throws away wrong matches, then affine, homography or TPS fits the shape. ALIGN-01, ALIGN-02
  8. Physics-based filteringReject matches that are physically impossible given terrain slope and sun angle. ALIGN-03
  9. Spread the points evenlyGrid top-k, refill empty cells, Delaunay coverage check. ALIGN-04 … ALIGN-07
  10. Sub-pixel refinementNCC peak fit, phase correlation, cornerSubPix, ECC, least squares, and a self-test with a known shift. PREC-01 … PREC-06
  11. Control gates and confidence tierNull tests, perturbation test, mask guard, scale check, then HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW / REJECTED. CHECK-01 … CHECK-10
  12. GATE B · RegistrationResult is handed over
  13. DeliverGeoTIFF, CSV/GeoJSON match points, the four metrics, HTML report, provenance, CLI, API and web demo. OUT-01 … OUT-10, UI-01 … UI-09

The three handoff objects are fixed in contracts.py: SceneMeta (what the label said), ImagePlane (one ready-to-match tile) and RegistrationResult (matches, transform, metrics, gates, tier, failures). Every metric defaults to None ("not measured"), never a made-up value.

8 · Benchmark & ablation: how we prove things

No existing benchmark fits lunar, multi-sensor data with public ground truth, so we build our own four-tier benchmark.

TierPairScaleSun-angle differenceWhat it proves
EasyOHRC ↔ LRO NAC, near the equator~2:1< 20°A sanity check. We should reproduce the published ~0.6 px class result.
MediumIIRS ↔ LRO WAC~1.25:120–60°A sanity check. We should reproduce the published sub-pixel result.
HardTMC-2 ↔ LRO NAC or TMC-2 ↔ SELENE TC10:1 / ~2:160°+This tier is our contribution. Nobody has done it on real data.
ExtremeOHRC ↔ IIRS~320:1anyIncluded to report the failure honestly, not to claim success.

Ground truth: we work out the true alignment two independent ways, from the label geometry and from a DEM (elevation map) re-projection. The disagreement between them is reported as the uncertainty. We never trust a single source.

The six-stage ablation (does each piece actually help?)

StageWhat we addWhat it tests
BaselineSIFT + RANSACThe field's standard floor. Expected to degrade at the poles.
Exp 1+ CLAHE / phase congruencyDoes image preparation help? Results split equatorial vs polar.
Exp 2+ learned matcher (LightGlue)How much does AI help? Checked against the published SuperGlue-vs-SIFT gap.
Exp 3 ★+ physics-based filterOur novel measurement. Inlier precision on repetitive crater fields.
Exp 4 ★+ scale-bridging cascadeThe difference between "0 inliers" and a working result on the Hard and Extreme tiers.
Exp 5+ sub-pixel refinementError below one pixel, measured by known-shift recovery.
FinalFull systemShould beat every stage on Hard/Extreme and at least match published numbers on Easy/Medium.

If a stage doesn't help, the report says so plainly. The ablation exists to test each piece, not to assume it works.

12 · Honest weak spots (know these before a judge finds them)

  • Nothing is measured yet. All features are TODO. Any number we show must come from our own runs.
  • The Hard tier may not work well. The best competitor reached 2.8% on TMC-2↔NAC. We report whatever we get; an honest weak number beats a fabricated strong one.
  • The crowd is big. About 49 teams are on this problem statement (the research scores crowding 4/10). We differentiate on the parts that are still open, not on UI polish.
  • The geodetic bridge (MATCH-11) is a hypothesis, not a proven method. Say so.
  • Sun-angle fields in real OHRC/TMC-2 labels are confirmed at the documentation level, not yet checked byte by byte. That check is a build step.
  • PRADAN login may be slow. We build first against no-login NASA/JAXA data and real committed label samples.
  • ISIS3 is fragile for OHRC, so the pipeline is designed to work without it.
  • "You just glued libraries together." That's true, and it's deliberate. See question 41.
  • Explainability is partial. The classical/RIFT path is interpretable; the learned path is not, and we don't pretend otherwise.

13 · Glossary

Registration
Lining up two images of the same place so the same points overlap.
Source / reference
Source = the image we move (Chandrayaan-2). Reference = the fixed trusted image (LRO / SELENE).
GSD
Ground sample distance: metres of ground per pixel. Smaller is sharper.
Scale ratio
How many times bigger one camera's pixel is than the other's, e.g. OHRC:IIRS ≈ 320.
Keypoint
A distinctive spot (a crater rim, a boulder corner) a matcher can find again.
Descriptor
A list of numbers describing what the area around a keypoint looks like.
Match / inlier / outlier
A match pairs a point in each image. An inlier is a correct match that fits the transform; an outlier is a wrong one.
Inlier ratio
Inliers ÷ all candidate matches, from 0 to 1. The quickest sign of how hard a pair is.
RMSE
Root mean square error: the average leftover distance of inliers after alignment, in pixels and metres.
Sub-pixel
Accuracy finer than one pixel, e.g. 0.3 px.
RANSAC / MAGSAC++
Fit the transform on random small groups of matches and keep the one most matches agree with. MAGSAC++ removes the hand-tuned threshold.
Affine / homography / TPS
Transform shapes: shift-rotate-scale-shear; plus perspective; plus smooth local bending.
PDS3 / PDS4
NASA's planetary data formats. PDS4 uses an XML label; PDS3 uses a text .lbl label.
Label
The metadata file describing an image: size, pixel type, location, sun angles, time.
Panchromatic / hyperspectral
Panchromatic = one black-and-white band. Hyperspectral = hundreds of narrow colour bands.
Incidence / emission / phase angle
Sun to ground; ground to camera; the angle sun–ground–camera. They describe the lighting and viewing.
DEM
Digital elevation model: a height map of the ground.
Slope / aspect
How steep the ground is, and which direction it faces.
SPICE
NASA's system for spacecraft position, pointing and sun position at any moment.
CLAHE
Contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalisation: boosts contrast region by region.
Phase congruency
An edge and structure map built from wave phase rather than brightness, so it survives lighting and sensor changes.
Footprint
The outline of the ground an image covers, from its corner lat/lon.
Regime
The situation type (normal light, polar light, cross-sensor, extreme scale) that decides which matcher runs.
Cascade
Crossing a big resolution gap in several smaller matching steps.
Control gate
A built-in test (e.g. matching a blank image) that must fail. If it "succeeds", the pipeline is broken.
ship_mode
A config switch, on by default, that blocks non-commercial models from the delivered software.
